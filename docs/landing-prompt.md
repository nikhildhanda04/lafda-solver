# Design the landing page for Love Lafda Solver

Design one page: the public front page at `/`. Nothing else. The app screens
behind the link already exist and are not yours to touch.

---

## What the product is

Two people in an argument each write their side privately. Neither can see the
other's words at any point. When both are finished and have locked their
submission, one AI mediator reads both accounts and returns what they already
agree on plus a concrete proposed resolution.

"Lafda" is Hindi slang for a fight or a mess.

## The one rule everything hangs off

Writing blind. The moment one person reads the other's version they start
rebutting instead of explaining, so the product never lets that happen. It is
the only structural rule in the product, and it is the only thing a competitor
cannot copy by adding a feature. Everything else — the steps, the modes, the
mediator — exists to support it.

## Who is reading this page

Someone mid-argument. Defensive, convinced they are right, probably on a phone,
often late at night. They did not come browsing; someone sent them here, or they
searched for something like "settle an argument". They are deciding in about
fifteen seconds whether this is a toy, a therapy upsell, or something that will
actually produce an answer.

The second reader is the other person in the argument, who was sent a link and
wants to know what they have been dragged into before clicking it.

---

## What the page has to get across

- It takes two people, writing separately. You cannot use it alone.
- Neither of you sees the other until you have both locked. Nothing leaks — not a
  word, not a timestamp, not whether they have started.
- There are five steps: opening statement, what happened, proposed solution,
  evidence, then review and lock.
- Locking is final.
- A resolution always comes back, even when nobody is found at fault.
- A verdict on who was right is optional, chosen when the case is created, and the
  mediator often declines to give one.
- No account, no email, no login. You get two links. Those links are the only keys
  and cannot be recovered.
- It is one argument, one outcome. No history, no dashboard, no ongoing relationship
  with the product.

## What the page must do

Create a case. That is the only action: a title for the argument, and a choice
between "common ground only" and "common ground and a verdict". Two links come
back, one for each person.

---

## Constraints

- No real-world photography of people. There are no customers to quote and no
  team to show. Do not invent testimonials, logos, user counts or press mentions.
- Nothing that implies an account, a dashboard, a subscription, or a list of past
  cases — none of those exist.
- Mobile matters at least as much as desktop.
- Do not promise therapy, counselling, or legal standing. It is not any of those.
- If a case involves possible abuse or self-harm the product withholds the verdict
  entirely and shows help instead. The page should not be cheerful in a way that
  the product would have to contradict.

## Tone currently in the product

Plain, unhurried, slightly formal. It does not joke about the argument and does not
perform empathy. Its vocabulary is mediation, not therapy or chat: mediator,
statement, evidence, lock, resolution, common ground. You may argue for a different
register — just say so and commit to it.

Decide the structure, the look, the words and the order yourself.
